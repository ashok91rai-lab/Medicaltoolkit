Understanding Calories — How Many Do You Really Need?

Calories are the units of energy your body uses to perform every function — from breathing and circulation to exercise and digestion. Your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) represents the total number of calories your body burns in a day, accounting for your resting metabolism and all physical activity. Understanding your TDEE is the foundation of any effective weight management strategy. Eating below your TDEE creates a calorie deficit for weight loss; eating above it creates a surplus for muscle gain. This calculator uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, published in 1990 and validated across diverse populations, which is considered the most accurate formula for estimating BMR in the general population. The formula takes into account your weight, height, age, and sex to estimate your resting calorie burn, which is then multiplied by an activity factor to give your TDEE.

How to Use the Calorie Calculator

  • Step 1 — Enter your age, sex, height, and weight: These are used to calculate your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) — the calories your body burns at complete rest.
  • Step 2 — Select your activity level: Be honest — most people overestimate their activity. "Sedentary" applies to desk jobs with little or no deliberate exercise. "Lightly Active" means 1–3 days of light exercise per week.
  • Step 3 — Click "Calculate Calories" to see your BMR, TDEE (maintenance calories), and calorie targets for weight loss and weight gain.

Start by eating at your TDEE for 2 weeks and monitor your weight. This confirms whether the calculation is accurate for you. Then adjust by 300–500 calories toward your goal.

How Your Calories Are Calculated — The Formula

BMR for Men:
(10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5

BMR for Women:
(10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier:
Sedentary: × 1.2 | Lightly Active: × 1.375 | Moderately Active: × 1.55
Very Active: × 1.725 | Extremely Active: × 1.9

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is accurate to within ±10% for most individuals. Athletes and highly muscular individuals may find their actual TDEE runs 10–15% higher than calculated due to higher muscle mass metabolic contribution.

Understanding Your Calorie Results

  • BMR: Calories burned at complete rest — breathing, circulation, and organ function. Represents 60–75% of total calorie expenditure for most people.
  • TDEE (Maintenance): Your BMR × activity factor. Eat at this level to maintain current weight over time.
  • Weight Loss (−500 cal/day): A 500-calorie deficit leads to approximately 1 lb (0.45 kg) of fat loss per week. Do not eat below 1,200 cal/day (women) or 1,500 cal/day (men) without medical supervision.
  • Lean Gain (+300 cal/day): A modest surplus with consistent resistance training promotes lean muscle growth while minimizing fat gain.

Calorie Quality — What You Eat Matters As Much As How Much

  • Protein (4 cal/g): Aim for 0.7–1.0g per pound of body weight. Protein preserves muscle during weight loss, increases fullness, and has the highest thermic effect — 20–30% of protein calories are burned during digestion itself.
  • Complex Carbohydrates (4 cal/g): Prioritize whole grains, legumes, vegetables, and fruit over refined carbohydrates and added sugars. Fiber-rich foods increase satiety and support gut health.
  • Healthy Fats (9 cal/g): Essential for hormone production and vitamin absorption. Prioritize unsaturated fats (avocado, nuts, olive oil, fatty fish) over saturated fats.
  • Track for 2–4 weeks: Using a food tracking app for even a short period dramatically increases calorie awareness. Research shows most people underestimate their food intake by 20–40%.
  • NEAT matters: Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis — fidgeting, walking, standing — can burn 200–1,000+ extra calories daily. Increasing daily steps from 5,000 to 10,000 creates a meaningful caloric difference without formal exercise.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) is the number of calories your body burns at complete rest to maintain basic life functions — breathing, circulation, cell production, and temperature regulation. It represents approximately 60–75% of total daily calorie expenditure for most people.

What is the Mifflin-St Jeor formula?

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation (1990) is widely considered the most accurate BMR formula for the general population. For men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5. For women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161.

How accurate is a calorie calculator?

Calorie calculators provide estimates, not exact figures. Individual variations in metabolism, gut microbiome, hormone levels, and body composition mean actual needs can vary by 10–20% from calculated values. Use the result as a starting point and adjust based on real-world results over 2–4 weeks.

How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

A deficit of 500 calories per day below your TDEE typically produces approximately 0.5 kg (1 lb) of fat loss per week. A 1,000 calorie deficit targets roughly 1 kg per week. Do not go below 1,200 cal/day for women or 1,500 cal/day for men without medical supervision.
