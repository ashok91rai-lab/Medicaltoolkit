Understanding Heart Rate Training Zones

Training within specific heart rate zones allows you to target different physiological adaptations during exercise. The classic five-zone model divides exercise intensity based on percentage of maximum heart rate (Max HR). Your Max HR is estimated using the widely-used formula: 220 − age. Zone 1 (50–60%) is gentle recovery work. Zone 2 (60–70%) is the fat-burning aerobic base zone. Zone 3 (70–80%) develops cardiovascular fitness. Zone 4 (80–90%) pushes your lactate threshold. Zone 5 (90–100%) is maximum sprint effort. Training across all zones creates a well-rounded fitness programme. The optional Karvonen method refines zones using your resting heart rate for even more personalised targets.

How to Use the Heart Rate Calculator

  • Step 1 — Enter your age in years. The maximum heart rate formula is age-based.
  • Step 2 — Optionally enter your resting heart rate (RHR) in beats per minute. Measure RHR by counting your pulse for 60 seconds first thing in the morning before getting out of bed. This enables the more accurate Karvonen (Heart Rate Reserve) method for zone calculations.

Use a heart rate monitor, fitness band, or smartwatch during exercise to stay within your target zones. Accurate zone training is far more effective than exercising at a random intensity.

Heart Rate Formulas Explained

Maximum Heart Rate (MHR):
Tanaka Formula (2001): MHR = 208 − (0.7 × Age) [most accurate for adults]
Classic Formula: MHR = 220 − Age [widely used, ±10–20 bpm variation]

Karvonen Method (Target HR with RHR):
Target HR = ((MHR − RHR) × Intensity%) + RHR

Example: Age 35, RHR 60 → MHR = 183.5 bpm
Zone 2 (60–70%): ((183.5 − 60) × 0.65) + 60 = 140 bpm

The Karvonen method is preferred by exercise physiologists because it personalizes zones based on your individual cardiovascular fitness (reflected by RHR). A lower RHR means a higher Heart Rate Reserve, which shifts zones upward.

The 5 Heart Rate Training Zones

Heart rate zones define the intensity of cardiovascular exercise and determine what physiological adaptations occur:

  • Zone 1 — Very Light (50–60% MHR): Active recovery. Promotes blood flow, reduces soreness, enhances fat metabolism. Ideal for warm-up, cool-down, and recovery days.
  • Zone 2 — Light (60–70% MHR): The "fat burning zone." Primarily uses fat as fuel. Builds aerobic base, improves mitochondrial density, and is sustainable for long durations. Critical for endurance athletes.
  • Zone 3 — Moderate (70–80% MHR): Aerobic conditioning. Improves cardiovascular efficiency, increases VO2max, and builds stamina. A good default for general fitness.
  • Zone 4 — Hard (80–90% MHR): Anaerobic threshold training. Increases lactate threshold — the point at which exercise becomes uncomfortable. Dramatically improves speed and performance.
  • Zone 5 — Maximum (90–100% MHR): Maximum effort sprints. Builds peak power and speed. Only sustainable for short intervals (10–60 seconds). Not appropriate for beginners or those with cardiovascular conditions.

Heart Health: What Your Resting Heart Rate Reveals

Your resting heart rate (RHR) is one of the most powerful and easy-to-measure indicators of cardiovascular health:

  • 60–100 bpm: Normal adult range. Higher end may indicate stress, deconditioning, or health issues worth discussing with a doctor.
  • 40–60 bpm: Athletic range. Trained endurance athletes commonly have RHRs in this range, indicating highly efficient cardiac output.
  • Above 100 bpm (tachycardia): Can indicate dehydration, anemia, thyroid issues, heart arrhythmia, or anxiety. Seek medical evaluation if consistently elevated.
  • Below 40 bpm: May be normal in elite athletes but can also indicate bradycardia requiring cardiac evaluation in non-athletes.
  • Trend matters: A rising RHR over weeks may signal overtraining, illness, or increased stress. Track your RHR daily using a fitness tracker to identify these patterns early.

Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the 220 − age formula?

The 220 − age formula is a population average with a standard deviation of about ±10–12 bpm. It works well for most people but can overestimate max HR in older adults and underestimate it in very fit individuals. An actual max HR test (supervised exercise test) provides a more accurate measurement.

What is the best zone for fat burning?

Zone 2 (60–70% of max HR) is traditionally called the "fat burning zone" because fat is the primary fuel source at this intensity. However, higher intensity zones burn more total calories per minute, which may be more effective for overall fat loss. Zone 2 training also builds the aerobic base needed for all other training.

What is the Karvonen method?

The Karvonen heart rate reserve (HRR) method uses your resting HR to personalise training zones: Target HR = Resting HR + (HRR × intensity%). Where HRR = Max HR − Resting HR. This produces slightly higher zone targets than the standard % of Max HR method.

💓 Heart Rate Tips

Zone 2 is underrated Most recreational exercisers train too hard. 80% of your training should be in Zone 2 (conversational pace) for maximum long-term fitness gains.
HRV is even better Heart Rate Variability (HRV) — the variation between beats — is a more sensitive measure of recovery and fitness than RHR alone. Many smartwatches now track HRV.
Medication note Beta-blockers and other heart medications directly lower heart rate. If you take these, standard MHR formulas will not apply — consult your cardiologist for exercise guidance.
