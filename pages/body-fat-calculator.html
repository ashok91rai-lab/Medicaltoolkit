Body Fat Calculator — US Navy Method

Estimate your body fat percentage using the clinically validated US Navy method. Enter your measurements to get your body fat %, ACE category, and actionable health guidance.

Why Body Fat Percentage Matters More Than Weight Alone

Body weight alone doesn't tell the full story of your health. Two people of the same height and weight can have very different health profiles depending on their ratio of muscle to fat. Body fat percentage is a far more informative metric because it distinguishes between lean mass (muscle, bone, organs) and fat mass. The US Navy method is a simple, widely-used anthropometric formula that estimates body fat from waist, neck, and (for women) hip circumferences alongside height. While not as accurate as DEXA scans or hydrostatic weighing, it provides a reliable estimate that can be tracked over time to monitor fitness progress and health risk.

How to Use the Body Fat Calculator

This calculator uses the U.S. Navy Circumference Method — a validated technique used by the United States military for fitness assessments. It requires measurements of specific body circumferences alongside height and weight.

  • Step 1 — Select your sex: The measurement sites differ for men and women because fat distribution patterns differ physiologically.
  • Step 2 — Enter your height (in cm or inches).
  • Step 3 — Measure your waist: For men, measure at the navel. For women, measure at the narrowest point. Use a soft tape measure and breathe normally — do not suck in.
  • Step 4 — Men only — measure your neck: Measure at the narrowest point, just below the larynx (Adam's apple), angling slightly downward at the front.
  • Step 5 — Women only — measure your hips: Measure at the widest point across the buttocks.
  • Step 6 — Click Calculate to see your estimated body fat percentage, lean mass, fat mass, and fitness category.

For best accuracy, take measurements in the morning before eating, and take 3 measurements at each site and average them. This method has an accuracy of approximately ±3–4%.

The U.S. Navy Body Fat Formula

The U.S. Navy method uses logarithmic calculations based on circumference measurements to estimate body fat percentage:

For Men:
BF% = 86.010 × log₁₀(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log₁₀(height) + 36.76

For Women:
BF% = 163.205 × log₁₀(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log₁₀(height) − 78.387

All measurements in centimeters or inches (consistent units required)

Lean body mass = Total body weight × (1 − BF% / 100). Fat mass = Total body weight × (BF% / 100).

Body Fat Percentage Categories

Body fat percentage classifications are based on guidelines from the American Council on Exercise (ACE) and the World Health Organization (WHO):

  • Essential Fat — Men: 2–5%, Women: 10–13%: Minimum fat required for physiological functioning, including hormone production and organ protection. Below these levels is medically dangerous.
  • Athletes — Men: 6–13%, Women: 14–20%: Typical range for competitive athletes and highly trained individuals. Associated with excellent cardiovascular fitness.
  • Fitness — Men: 14–17%, Women: 21–24%: Lean and fit individuals. Associated with good health and athletic performance.
  • Acceptable — Men: 18–24%, Women: 25–31%: Healthy range for most adults. Slightly higher health risk than the fitness category but generally acceptable.
  • Obese — Men: 25%+, Women: 32%+: Excess body fat associated with increased risk of metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and joint problems.

Practical Tips to Reduce Body Fat

  • Calorie Deficit: A deficit of 500 calories/day leads to approximately 1 lb (0.45 kg) of fat loss per week. Do not exceed a 1,000 calorie/day deficit, as this increases muscle loss.
  • Prioritize Protein: Aim for 0.7–1.0 g of protein per pound of body weight. High protein intake preserves lean muscle mass during fat loss and increases satiety.
  • Resistance Training: Lifting weights 3–4 times per week builds and preserves lean mass, which raises your resting metabolic rate. More muscle = more calories burned at rest.
  • Progressive Cardio: HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training) burns significantly more calories in less time than steady-state cardio and continues to elevate metabolism for hours afterward.
  • Sleep 7–9 Hours: Sleep deprivation elevates cortisol (a fat-storing hormone), increases hunger hormones (ghrelin), and reduces the fat-burning hormone leptin. Poor sleep is one of the most underestimated obstacles to fat loss.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy body fat percentage?

For men: 6–17% is the athletic to fitness range; 18–24% is average. For women: 14–24% is athletic to fitness range; 25–31% is average. Above 25% (men) or 32% (women) is considered obese by ACE guidelines.

How accurate is the US Navy method?

The US Navy method is accurate to within about 3–4% compared to DEXA scans. It's reliable for tracking changes over time. For precision (e.g., clinical or competitive use), DEXA or hydrostatic weighing is recommended.

Where should I measure my waist for this calculator?

For men: measure at the navel level. For women: measure at the narrowest point of the torso (natural waist). Take measurements relaxed, not sucking in, at the end of a normal exhale.

Scale weight vs body fat You can lose fat and gain muscle simultaneously, making the scale stay the same while your body composition dramatically improves. Measure body fat, not just weight.
Visceral fat matters most Fat stored around abdominal organs (visceral fat) is more metabolically harmful than subcutaneous fat. A waist circumference over 40 inches (men) or 35 inches (women) signals elevated risk.
Retest every 4–6 weeks Body fat changes slowly. Measure every 4–6 weeks for meaningful comparisons. Daily fluctuations from hydration can be 1–2%.
